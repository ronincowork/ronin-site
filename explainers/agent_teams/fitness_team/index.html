Use case · Agent teams

The fitness team.

Have your agent be a head coach. It stays your coach across every session — what changes is the work in front of it: today's meal plan, this week's workouts, a recovery day, new shoes, the spring race.

job role: Head Coach — who the agent remains, all season long

session task: race planning — what it is doing at this moment

Head Coach is a role you define in your own Ronin — the same way the built-in Developer role is defined, but yours.

01

The role: a coach that stays your coach

The Head Coach's briefing is where your training philosophy lives: how you like to train, what your history is, what the coach should push on and what it should leave alone. Because the role is fixed for the session's life, that grounding never drifts — the agent answering about tomorrow's intervals is the same coach that planned your season.

02

The tasks: one coach, five kinds of work

Each task carries its own working notes — how you track meals, where the training log lives, what gear you already own. The coach switches task as the conversation moves, and picks up the right notes each time:

nutrition Meal planning and fueling, matched to the training load.
workouts Design the sessions — intervals, strength, long days.
recovery Manage rest, sleep, and the easy days that make hard days work.
equipment Choose and track gear — shoes, bike fit, what to replace when.
race planning Pick the goal race and build the season backward from it.
03

Why the split matters

A single "fitness assistant" prompt goes stale the moment the conversation changes subject. Splitting who the coach is from what it is doing right now keeps both sharp: the role holds what is always true about your training, and each task brings only the notes that kind of work needs. The same task can even serve another role later — planning is planning, whichever hat is worn.
