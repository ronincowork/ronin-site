Use case · Agent teams

The personal assistant team.

A deliberately narrow team: one assistant that works for you — your questions, your facts, your day. What makes it worth a seat is that it keeps what you tell it, in files on your machine, across every session, so you never start from zero.

family role: Assistant — the seats that answer rather than build

session task: personal assistant

Both ship with Ronin Cowork. The memory is plain files in the assistant's own folder, yours to read, edit and keep.

01

It works for a person, not a codebase

Every other team on this site points at work — a repository, a training plan, a household. This one points at you. Ask it who somebody is, what you decided last month, when the car is due, what to get your sister — the assistant's whole job is your questions, your facts, your day.

It answers rather than builds: it checks what it can actually check, names what it used, and says it does not know rather than guessing.

02

Its memory is files you own

An assistant without memory is a search box. This one writes down what you choose to keep and reads it back before it answers:

read first Before answering, it checks its notes — your facts beat its guesses.
keep on request "Remember this" writes it down. Never secrets, credentials, or others' work.
outlives sessions Sessions are mortal; the files are not. Tomorrow's assistant knows what you told today's.

The notes live in the assistant's folder on your machine, as plain documents you can open on its tile, edit, or delete.

03

Where else it can look

Start with nothing but what you tell it. When you want it to see more, drop files into its folder (exported mail, a calendar, a folder of notes), or connect an outside service as an MCP server and the assistant reaches it through Ronin, with your keys staying with Ronin. Anything that opens an outside connection happens one confirmed step at a time.

04

Narrow by design

A team does not have to be big to earn its place. This one is a single seat with a single task, and that is the point: the assistant sits on your Roster beside the developer team and the rest, always the same seat, accumulating your context instead of resetting it. The same model that runs six developer tasks runs one assistant — a family role, its task, and the briefing that makes it yours.
