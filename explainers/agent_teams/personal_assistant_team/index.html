Use case · Agent teams

The personal assistant team.

A deliberately narrow team: one assistant that works for you — your questions, your facts, your day. What makes it worth a seat is the brain behind it: gbrain remembers what you tell it, across every session, so you never start from zero.

family role: Assistant — the seats that answer rather than build

session task: personal assistant — powered by gbrain

Both ship with Ronin Cowork. The memory comes from gbrain, Garry Tan's open-source agent brain, connected through Ronin Services.

01

It works for a person, not a codebase

Every other team on this site points at work — a repository, a training plan, a household. This one points at you. Ask it who somebody is, what you decided last month, when the car is due, what to get your sister — the assistant's whole job is your questions, your facts, your day.

It answers rather than builds: it checks what it can actually check, names what it used, and says it does not know rather than guessing.

02

The integration: gbrain is the memory

An assistant without memory is a search box. This one works brain-first, against gbrain — an open-source agent brain that stores what you choose to keep and hands it back to any connected session:

search first Before answering, it searches gbrain — your facts beat its guesses.
capture on request "Remember this" stores it. Never secrets, credentials, or others' work.
outlives sessions Sessions are mortal; the brain is not. Tomorrow's assistant knows what you told today's.

The seat is born connected to its brain — an assistant defined by its memory is never launched with the door to it closed. Anything that opens an outside connection happens one confirmed step at a time, and if gbrain is unreachable it says so plainly and carries on as a plain assistant.

03

Where the brain gets its information

In Ronin you talk to the brain through your agent — every recall and every capture goes via the session. The brain itself is filled from the sides: gbrain ships connection recipes for the places your life already writes itself down, each set up on the brain with your own accounts and keys. Start with none, and add the ones you actually use:

what you tell it "Remember this" in any connected session files it straight into the brain. Works from day one, no setup.
your notes Markdown you already have — bulk-import a folder of notes and the brain indexes it.
Gmail Email flows into brain pages, so the assistant recalls who wrote what and what is still open.
Google Calendar Events become searchable pages — attendees, locations, and meeting-prep context by day.
meeting transcripts Circleback meeting notes auto-import, with attendee detection built in.
X (Twitter) Timeline, mentions, and keyword monitoring flow into the brain as pages.
voice Talk to the brain out loud — conversations land as pages it can recall later. a gbrain capability · not yet wired into Ronin

The recipes live in gbrain's own library and are documented there — see the gbrain repository for the full, current list.

04

Narrow by design

A team does not have to be big to earn its place. This one is a single seat with a single task, and that is the point: the assistant sits on your Roster beside the developer team and the rest, always the same seat, accumulating your context instead of resetting it. The same model that runs six developer tasks runs one assistant — a family role, its task, and the briefing that makes it yours.
